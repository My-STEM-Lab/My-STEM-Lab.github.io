## The test

Two papers, each **20 multiple-choice questions in 75 minutes**, with **no calculator**.
The two scores combine into a single result reported from **1.0 to 9.0**. There's no pass mark —
universities set their own expectations, and a strong score can turn into a reduced offer.

- **Paper 1** — applying familiar A-Level mathematics to unfamiliar situations
- **Paper 2** — mathematical reasoning: logic, proof, implication, counterexamples

Requirements change every admissions cycle, so always check your specific course pages.
Broadly, it's used by Cambridge, Imperial, Warwick, Durham, LSE and a growing group of others.

## What we work on

**Speed without carelessness.** 75 minutes for 20 questions sounds generous until you meet a
question that could eat fifteen of them. The skill is recognising that question and leaving it.

**Eliminating rather than solving.** These are multiple choice. Substituting a convenient value,
checking a limiting case, or testing the sign of an expression will often kill three options in
twenty seconds. Solving forwards is frequently the slow route.

**Paper 2 properly.** This is the paper most students under-prepare, because nothing in A-Level
trains it. Necessary versus sufficient, the negation of a statement with a quantifier, spotting
the exact line where a "proof" goes wrong — these are learnable, and quickly, but only if you
practise them deliberately.

**A common trap:** students prepare for the TMUA by doing more A-Level questions. It doesn't work.
The content is almost entirely familiar — the difficulty is in the reasoning and the pace.
Prepare with real past papers from the start.

## How preparation usually runs

Six to ten weeks, one session a week, with timed practice between sessions. We start with a full
paper cold to get a baseline, then alternate: one session on reasoning technique, one on reviewing
a timed paper question by question. Every error gets classified — gap, too slow, careless slip,
or misread — because the pattern tells us what to do next far better than the score does.
