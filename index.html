How I teach

Small, meaningful steps

Most students get stuck not because a topic is too hard, but because the question in front of them doesn't look like the one they practised. That needs a different fix.

Break it down

Every hard problem is a stack of small ones. We take them apart until each step is one you could have found yourself.

Understand, don't memorise

A memorised method survives until the exam rewords the question. Knowing why it works survives anything.

Work on your actual weak spots

Every mistake gets classified — a real gap, too slow, a careless slip, a misread question. The pattern tells us more than a score does.

Before you ask

Common questions

How do online sessions actually work?

Video call with a shared digital whiteboard, so we can both write and both see the working build up. You keep the whole board afterwards.

Is the trial session really free?

Yes. {{ site.trial_length }}, no payment details taken, no obligation afterwards.

What should we bring to the first session?

A topic that's currently going badly, and a recent piece of marked work if you have one.

Do you set homework?

Usually, and it's reviewed at the start of the next session. For admissions tests it's always timed, because untimed practice teaches the wrong habit.

How far ahead should we start admissions test preparation?

For TMUA and ESAT, six to ten weeks of focused work is usually enough. STEP wants far longer — ideally from Year 12 or the summer before Year 13.

Are the free resources really free?

Yes — no sign-up, no email address needed. Have a look at the resources page.
